01 / THE SIGNAL

What is speech to an ALM?

One utterance is a layered signal. An ALM may preserve and react to all three layers.

C1
Aa

Semantics

What is said

The linguistic content: phonemes, words, phrases, and sentences that carry the intended message.

C2
≋

Paralinguistic features

How it is said—and by whom

Emotion, prosody, speaker identity, age, gender, accent, and other vocal characteristics beyond the words.

C3
⌁

Channel & acoustics

Where and how it was recorded

Background noise, transmission artifacts, speaking rate, spectral shifts, and adversarial perturbations.

Same words + different cues = a meaningfully different input to an ALM.

02 / THE CRITERIA

What makes an ALM trustworthy?

TRACE connects each family of audio cues to a core dimension of trustworthy behavior.

C1 · Linguistic

Safety

Whether harmful spoken requests are recognized and refused while benign requests remain answerable.

Does the model respond safely to what was said?
C2 · Paralinguistic

Fairness

Whether responses remain consistent across speaker demographics, identities, and emotional expression.

Does the same request receive the same treatment?
C3 · Channel

Robustness

Whether behavior stays reliable under acoustic degradation, channel noise, and adversarial perturbations.

Does the model remain dependable when audio changes?

03 / THE PIPELINES

How do ALMs listen?

Audio reaches the language model through three architectural paradigms, each preserving different information.

01

Cascaded

Speech is transcribed first. The language model operates on text, so most speaker and acoustic information is removed.

Audio→ASR→Text→LLM
02

Latent based

A speech encoder projects audio into continuous representations, preserving acoustic and paralinguistic details beyond words.

Audio→Encoder→Adapter→LLM
03

Audio token based

Speech becomes discrete audio tokens processed natively, entangling semantics, emotion, identity, and channel conditions.

Audio→Tokenizer→Audio tokens→LLM
